# ReWear should measure grade yield, not kilograms

The sharpest finding in this research is commercial, not technical: in second-hand textile sorting, premium "cream" grade accounts for roughly **10% of sorted weight but over 50% of a sorting centre's revenue**, while wearable fractions overall generate **more than 90% of income** ([Oxford Economics 2024, p.19](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/sites/default/files/2025-02/OE_SHC-Socioeconomic-Impact-Study.pdf)). ReWear WMS currently sets and bonuses against *daily processed-input targets* — kilograms in — which is the less important variable. That single mismatch reframes the whole roadmap: the application's job is not to make the line faster but to make grade-mix decisions visible, attributable and defensible. Against the published 2025 baseline for a single-site operation of this size, ReWear is genuinely ahead on the things custom systems usually get wrong — immutable audit trails, exact-decimal money, quarantine instead of silent write-off, scan-every-package handover, checksum-verified backup with staged restore — and behind on three specific things: a purchase-lot entity for bought-in and imported feedstock, grade-yield measurement, and any confirmation step that happens at the physical location of the goods. Two gaps are not discretionary at all: the NAV Online Számla integration is a demonstration that sends nothing, against a penalty of **up to HUF 1,000,000 per invoice** since 1 August 2024 ([BDO Hungary](https://www.bdo.hu/hu-hu/aktualitasok-blog/blog/nav-online-szamla-adatszolgaltatas-kotelezettsegek,-hataridok,-bejovo-szamlak)), and weights are still floating point in a business whose EPR fine is **half the fee deficiency plus the outstanding fee**, assessed on mass in kilograms ([EU-PRO](https://eupro.hu/epr-dij-2026-dijtetelek/)). A caution runs through everything below: much of the published evidence in this field is vendor marketing, paywalled or absent, and this report marks where it is.

## Audit discipline is this application's unusual strength, and the floor is its structural weakness

Start with what ReWear does better than the products it would be compared against. Gartner's 2025 commentary is that core WMS function has reached **near-parity across vendors**, so differentiation has moved to the value-added layer — labour management, task interleaving, yard and dock scheduling ([Softeon, quoting the 2025 Gartner MQ](https://www.softeon.com/?p=5201)). That matters because it sets the right question for a custom build. The post-mortem literature is explicit that the recurring failure of in-house systems is not module count but exception handling: test scripts cover normal functionality while "exceptions" and "user errors" are the items that "tend to be overlooked", and design documentation skews to happy paths so that "the exceptions such as picking/packing are overlooked compared to happy paths and not documented well during the design phase" ([Tompkins](https://tompkinsinc.com/post/navigating-the-pitfalls-of-a-warehouse-management-system-implementation-part-one)). Judged on exactly that axis, ReWear is strong. Damaged stock moves to a QUARANTINED status for a human to return or write off; uncounted stock is reported missing and never written off automatically; invoice cancellation happens only through a structurally linked credit note; every status change carries the acting user. That is the "flag exceptions rather than guess" discipline that vendor guidance recommends for offline-capable systems and that ReWear arrived at independently ([Cleverence](https://www.cleverence.com/articles/business-blogs/how-to-offline-warehouse-4931/)).

The invoice lifecycle is better than merely tidy — it happens to match Hungarian law. A faulty invoice cannot be deleted; it must be cancelled (*sztornó*) or corrected (*módosító*), and both are themselves reportable documents ([Zappla](https://zappla.hu/tudastar/szamlazas-alapok-egyeni-vallalkozoknak)). The NAV v3.0 schema treats this as first-class, with `CREATE / MODIFY / STORNO / ANNUL` operations and an explicit correction chain of `originalInvoiceNumber` plus a monotonically increasing `modificationIndex` ([NAV EN v3.0 interface specification](https://onlineszamla.nav.gov.hu/api/files/container/download/Online%20Szamla_Interfesz%20specifikacio_EN_v3.0.pdf)). ReWear's state machine — draft to issued, issued or overdue to paid, paid back to issued, cancellation only via credit note — is the shape the regulator requires. Money stored as exact decimal text, separate ledgers per currency, partner ageing, P&L and stock valuation reporting is also unusually deep for an application of this size; this is ERP territory rather than WMS territory, and most SMB warehouse products do not have it.

Three further strengths deserve naming because they answer specific findings. First, the route-sorted pick list — zone, aisle, shelf, numeric-aware — attacks travel, which is the dominant component of pick time in an activity described as over half of picking-operation cost ([SelectHub](https://selecthub.com/warehouse-management/warehouse-management-software-features-requirements/)); zone-based walking order with pick-path sequencing is a **paid third-party add-on even in Odoo**, listed at $135.72 ([Odoo Apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/18.0/wave_batch_zone_picking)). Second, requiring *every* package to be scanned at handover before shipping is a stronger outbound control than production WMS defaults: Infor makes location confirmation mandatory only in putaway and configurable per zone in picking, with "no verification" among the options ([Infor WMS](https://docs.infor.com/wms/2026.x/en-us/useradminlib/scepickug/zfo1612893813206.html); [Infor putaway](https://docs.infor.com/wms/2022.x/en-us/useradminlib/sceputug/pwi1612894067799.html)). Third, 166 automated tests, a reproducible first-run setup and checksum-verified backup bundles with staged restore and rollback are a direct answer to the documented decay mode of homegrown systems, where "the availability of development resources to update and maintain the aging applications is reduced" and institutional knowledge walks out with one engineer ([Veridian](https://veridian.info/homegrown-wms); [NetSuite](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/supply-chain-management/5-ways-a-custom-built-warehouse-management-system-can-come-up-short.shtml)).

Now the honest other side. ReWear has 20 menu modules including internal company email with an attachment vault and an Element-style team chat. Over-buying is a recognised selection error — buyers acquire "a WMS with far more functionality than they realistically need" ([MH&L](https://www.mhlnews.com/technology-amp-automation/how-select-wms)) — and the equivalent error in a custom build is self-inflicted surface area. Email and chat are the modules most exposed to the maintenance-decay finding above, and they are not where the gaps are. Meanwhile the structural weakness is that the application is desktop-only, which means that for the supply driver, the delivery driver, the sorter and the station manager, the confirmation step happens at a screen rather than at the goods. The strongest evidence available here is Setayesh, Grosse, Glock and Neumann's review of 46 papers plus interviews with 38 order pickers across 14 companies in four countries, which identifies **memory demand, complexity, communication and vision** among ten human-factors failure modes and concludes they are "linked to OP system design, operation, and management" rather than individual carelessness ([IJPR 2022, 60(20):6350–6372](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/tprsxx/v60y2022i20p6350-6372.html)). Any step where a worker carries an item identity, a quantity or a location in their head from screen to shelf is a documented error source. It must be said plainly, though, that **no source was found that measures the productivity or error cost of a desktop-only warehouse system** — the case for a mobile client rests on that failure-mode mechanism plus worker-preference survey data, not on a measured cost. The best proxy figures are Zebra's, where **74% of associates say they spend too much time on tasks that could be automated** and 91% rate task management tools important ([Zebra, 2025](https://www.zebra.com/us/en/about-zebra/newsroom/press-releases/2025/70-of-frontline-workers-report-rising-concerns-with-injuries-on-the-warehouse-floor.html)) — and even those come from the press release and trade reporting because the underlying report PDF is encrypted and could not be verified.

One architectural observation that cuts against the obvious conclusion: ReWear's offline-only SQLite design is a genuine asset, because warehouse Wi-Fi failure is treated in practice as a routine event to design for rather than an exception ([ArcherPoint](https://archerpoint.com/?p=24284)). The application has the document cache by construction. The moment a handheld client appears, it acquires the whole synchronisation problem that currently does not exist — idempotent posting with retry so duplicate posts cannot occur, a client-generated unique id plus device, worker and timestamp on every floor transaction, queue visibility showing what is pending and which device needs attention, provisionally-known stock distinct from confirmed stock, and conflicts raised as exceptions rather than resolved by guesswork ([Cleverence](https://www.cleverence.com/articles/business-blogs/how-to-offline-warehouse-4931/)). That, not the user interface, is the real cost of going mobile, and every figure in that literature is vendor-published with no measured data at all.

## The gap that matters is grade mix and provenance, not module count

Measured against the convergent industry core — receiving against a purchase order or ASN, putaway, location management, inventory control and counting, replenishment, order allocation, picking, packing, shipping with carrier integration, returns, reporting and integration ([SelectHub](https://selecthub.com/warehouse-management/warehouse-management-software-features-requirements/)) — ReWear's absences divide cleanly into three classes: gaps that are correct scoping decisions for this trade, gaps that are real deficiencies in the core, and gaps the generic list does not even contain because they are specific to second-hand textiles.

The domain-specific gaps are where the real value sits, and the generic capability list contains none of them.

**Grade cannot be a character in an identifier.** ReWear encodes quality grade inside a structured QR string alongside date, target group, garment type, packaging, weight and a hex suffix. But facilities work to **between 90 and 500 different categories** depending on destination, with German practice reaching **350 fractions**, and they maintain "highly sophisticated sorting manuals per market and season" ([Oxford Economics, p.15](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/sites/default/files/2025-02/OE_SHC-Socioeconomic-Impact-Study.pdf); [TexFile](https://texfile.tech/knowledge/espr-epr-timeline-for-sorters)). There is no global standard: trade guides state plainly that "definitions vary by sorting facility, region, and market demand", so an "A" from one supplier may overlap another's "B" ([The Rag Depot Vintage](https://www.theragdepotvintage.com/post/understanding-bale-grades-in-the-used-clothing-trade)). Fashion for Good's own research had to abandon grade names entirely for a Black/Grey/White fraction scheme precisely because grades were not comparable between participating sorters, and its grey fraction "includes grades defined by each participating sorter based on their own market challenges" ([FFG Sorters Handbook §1.3](https://www.fashionforgood.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Sorting-For-Circularity-Europe_Sorters-Handbook_Fashion-for-Good.pdf)). The requirement that follows — and this is the researcher's inference, built on those findings rather than stated by any source — is that a grade must be a **named definition owned by the facility, versioned with an effective date, and mappable per customer contract**, because once the definition drifts, every historical package record becomes uninterpretable.

**There is no purchase lot, so there is no provenance.** This is the schema keystone. A purchase-lot entity upstream of sorting would carry supplier, country of origin, collection channel, purchase price per kilogram, gross and net weight, and customs classification. Each of those has a concrete consequence. Purchase price matters because the economics are brutally tight: post-consumer clothes collected in the EU27+ sold on average for **€0.50–0.65/kg in 2023** with surveyed sorting centres paying **€0.53/kg**, against a German sorting cost of about **€0.68/kg**, which means roughly €1.21/kg of cost must be recovered from a mix whose sale prices run from **€4.50/kg for cream** down to **€0.16/kg for recycling** and a mixed-waste tail that *costs* money to dispose of ([Oxford Economics, pp.13, 20–21](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/sites/default/files/2025-02/OE_SHC-Socioeconomic-Impact-Study.pdf); [TexFile](https://texfile.tech/knowledge/espr-epr-timeline-for-sorters)). Those prices need to be editable inputs rather than embedded constants, because the grade prices available date from 2016–2022 sources re-reported in 2024 and **current 2025–26 per-kilogram prices are a genuine gap in the research**. Customs classification matters because it is a per-lot business decision with money attached: Textrade has, since 2023, classified its inputs as "textile waste from the population" rather than "used clothing" because the classification affects customs duties ([ELTE KRTK](https://krtk.elte.hu/en/2026/01/new-legislations-and-challenges-in-europe-what-to-do-with-textile-waste/)). Collection channel matters because household-collected textiles fall under **Y46**, which requires prior written notification and consent even for intra-EU recovery shipments and whose recovery export to non-OECD countries is prohibited; mixing destroys the classification, so the channel must propagate from intake through sorting into the outbound lot ([LG Fashion Labs](https://lgfashionlabs.com/eu-waste-shipment-regulation-textile-exports.html)). And legal status matters most of all: separately collected used textiles are **waste from the moment of collection** unless directly handed over by an end user and "directly professionally assessed as fit for re-use at the collection point" ([Brubru, on Directive 2025/1892](https://brubru.beresol.eu/eucanon/2025-1892_wfd_textiles/)). That assessment is a human judgement made at intake, and a system that records only an aggregate weight and a free-text area cannot reconstruct it later — which is exactly what the export declaration turns on.

**Grade yield is the metric that is missing.** With wearables generating over 90% of income and cream over 50% of revenue on roughly 10% of weight, a percentage point of grade mix moves the business far more than a percentage point of throughput. ReWear reports graded-bag output and holds a movement log with operator attribution, so the raw material for yield reporting per intake lot, per team, per shift and per sorter is already in the database. What is absent is the computation and the target. Two honest caveats belong with this recommendation. The yield cascade itself — roughly 10% cream, 46% next-best including "tropical mix", 15% lower quality, 10% wipers, 8% mechanical recycling, 3% paper and plastic, 8% landfilled or incinerated — is Nordic data (Watson & Palm 2016, using 2014 figures) and **no yield figures specific to Hungarian or Central-European input were found**. And the reusable/recyclable/waste split varies widely by jurisdiction and input channel: 69/23/8 as a 2023 sorting-centre survey average, 53/33/14 in the Netherlands, 58/32/10 in France, 75–80/13–17/5–6 in Lithuania ([Oxford Economics, p.17](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/sites/default/files/2025-02/OE_SHC-Socioeconomic-Impact-Study.pdf)). The application should therefore learn the site's own baseline and report variance against it, never ship a target drawn from these numbers.

**Weights as floating point is now a control failure.** The developer already fixed money to exact decimal text; weight is the same class of problem with a regulator attached. The Hungarian EPR fee is quantity × rate in kilograms and the penalty for omitted or incorrect reporting is a fine equal to **half the fee deficiency plus the outstanding fee** ([EU-PRO](https://eupro.hu/epr-dij-2026-dijtetelek/)). The stocktake tolerance is 100 g. Trade contracts quote weight tolerances of ±3% to ±5% per bale — although every one of those tolerance figures is seller-published and **traces to no standard or study**, so they belong in a per-customer contract field, not a constant ([Vintage Supplier](https://vintagesupplier.com/vintage-used-branded-clothing-wholesale-guide/); [Alibaba 25 kg bale guide](https://apparel.alibaba.com/guide/25kg-used-clothes-bale-buyer%E2%80%99s-guide)). Mass accuracy in this business is a financial control.

**One thing not to build: per-garment capture.** Fashion for Good measured professional sorters at **41 seconds per garment** under a scan-plus-tablet-data-entry protocol, of which the near-infrared scan itself took one second — the rest was manual entry — and individual garments were **not weighed at all, only total bales were** ([FFG Sorters Handbook](https://www.fashionforgood.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Sorting-For-Circularity-Europe_Sorters-Handbook_Fashion-for-Good.pdf)). Any design that assumes item-level capture at the sorting station is modelling something that study found too slow to run in production. The widely-quoted "22 kilos per person per hour" from that handbook is the rate of that research protocol, **not production sorting throughput**, and must never be used as a target. Production rates themselves are poorly evidenced: the manual **100–150 kg per person per hour** figure comes from an MDPI paper the researcher could not fetch (HTTP 403) and reflects a search engine's reading of it, while the TEXAID/Avery Dennison figure of 2,400 kg per day is a vendor case study, and **no independent non-vendor throughput figure for a commercial European manual fine-sorting line was found** ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-7248/4/3/22); [Avery Dennison](https://www.averydennison.com/na/en/industries/apparel/perspectives/texaid-case-study)). Worse, the annualised arithmetic does not reconcile: TEXAID's Hungarian plant at Bélapátfalva sorts 3,600 t with 80 staff and Textrade reportedly 35,000 t with over 500, which is 45 and 70 tonnes per employee per year, or roughly **20–32 kg per employee-hour** — an order of magnitude below the line rates ([ELTE KRTK](https://krtk.elte.hu/en/2026/01/new-legislations-and-challenges-in-europe-what-to-do-with-textile-waste/)). Any capacity model built from published line rates will overstate real plant output by a large factor.

## Eleven roles are really three applications wearing one skin

The role hierarchy splits by where the person's hands are. Floor roles act on physical goods and need one decision per screen at the place of the goods. Line and shift management roles need working artefacts — a handover sheet, a shift grid, a labour plan, a bonus breakdown — not charts. Desk and compliance roles need immutable records and document packs. The sizes below are rough: *small* is a few days inside existing structures, *medium* is a new screen or table plus the logic around it, *large* is a schema change or a new client.

### Floor roles: sorter, sorting helper, supply driver, delivery driver, station manager

The **sorter (válogató)** calls for a line by name — never by weight, because the delivered amount is only known when the driver brings it — then sorts into graded bags against a daily processed-input target with a bonus rate. The research says their difficulty is judgement under ambiguity, not speed: categorisation "is a matter of subjective judgement of each individual sorter", accurate sorting "requires skilled and trained staff who adjust their sorting decisions with respect to different markets", and one interviewee estimated **around six months to become a good sorter** ([Oxford Economics, pp.13, 16](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/sites/default/files/2025-02/OE_SHC-Socioeconomic-Impact-Study.pdf); [FFG Sorters Handbook §2.3](https://www.fashionforgood.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Sorting-For-Circularity-Europe_Sorters-Handbook_Fashion-for-Good.pdf)). The sorter's second problem is fairness: if the system cannot separate stoppages, waiting for stock, cleaning and training a new starter from measured hours, every bonus figure will be contested, and transparency of calculation reduces disputes more than fast payment does ([FlowHCM](https://flowhcm.com/piece-rate-trap-in-payroll-management/)).

The **sorting helper (válogatósegéd)** packs graded bags and moves them to the labelling station. This is the role with the least software surface and the most physical load. The design stance the evidence converges on is severe simplicity: around 8% of men have red-green colour vision deficiency ([HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/PUBNS/web03.pdf)), some warehouse workers "have never used a computer system" ([SnapFulfil / SDC Exec](https://www.sdcexec.com/software-technology/article/22392812/snapfulfil-managing-change-for-a-smooth-technology-transition)), and physical and mental fatigue are two of the ten named failure modes. Note also that the real intervention for this role is not software: sorters and helpers "are required to stand for long hours", so orthopaedic mats, table-height adjustment, lighting and the placement of new and sorted bales are recommended line-design factors ([FFG Sorters Handbook](https://www.fashionforgood.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Sorting-For-Circularity-Europe_Sorters-Handbook_Fashion-for-Good.pdf)).

The **supply forklift driver (alapanyag-targoncás)** stores arriving lines before they become available, then answers raw requests and delivers lines to stations. The **delivery forklift driver (kiszállítási targoncás)** picks to the route-sorted list, loads at the assigned dispatch dock and scans every package at handover. Both spend their shift at the wheel while the application sits on a desk. For them the Infor pattern is instructive: location confirmation is **mandatory** in putaway and the zone-level "no verification" option does not apply, which tells you the task is expected to happen at the rack face ([Infor WMS](https://docs.infor.com/wms/2022.x/en-us/useradminlib/sceputug/pwi1612894067799.html)). The sharper principle, synthesised from the check-digit literature and Nielsen Norman Group's work on confirmation fatigue, is that **confirmations must be information-bearing**: a confirmation a worker can satisfy without looking at the goods adds friction without accuracy and trains dismissal, and any predictable token is defeated by habituation — a spoken or printed check digit "can be memorised and read back without the picker being at the correct slot" ([US 8868519B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8868519B2); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-mistakes/)). ReWear's handover scan already meets that bar. Its putaway and pick steps do not.

The **station manager (állomásvezető)** registers and labels packaged output. This is the single most consequential role for compliance, because it is where a graded bag becomes an identified package and where the legally required artefacts are either produced or lost. Every shipment of used textiles fit for re-use needs a sorting record **fixed securely but not permanently to the packaging**, describing items at the most detailed sorting level used — type, size, colour, gender, material composition — and naming the company that carried out the final sorting with its address ([LG Fashion Labs, on Art. 22d(9)](https://lgfashionlabs.com/eu-waste-shipment-regulation-textile-exports.html)). ReWear's QR code carries date, target group, garment type, quality grade, packaging, weight and a hex suffix — adjacent to, but not the same as, that field set, and there is no printable non-permanent bale record. A caveat the developer should act on before building: those article numbers come from a commercial summary page, the full Annex VII field list was not obtained, and the Commission delegated act on completing the Annex VII document — due 21 May 2026 — could not be confirmed as adopted. **Verify against EUR-Lex before writing the label template.**

### Line and shift management: production manager, shift leader, warehouse manager

The **production manager (termelési vezető)** owns the sorting operation — targets, team composition, the daily plan, bonus approval — and this is where the single highest-value change in the whole roadmap sits: replacing kilograms-in as the primary measure with grade yield per intake lot, team, shift and sorter. The second change is less obvious but equally leveraged: a downtime and non-productive-time log. Lean practice is specific about how to build one. Keep the reason-code list short and fixed — one guide recommends **eight loss groups with fewer than ten reasons each** — because plants running 200-item lists on touchscreens during active stoppages get "operators who pick the first plausible option", and the list must be maintained in the operators' own words and reviewed every two weeks ([Fabrico](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/packaging-downtime-codes/)). A single vendor source claims that **without a coding standard 38% of downtime is logged as "unknown"**; that figure is unverified and should be treated as illustrative ([iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/blog/downtime-classification-standards-accurate-oee)). Two things make this feature unusually efficient for ReWear. First, sorting is classified in OEE terms as a **quality-related loss consuming line time**, with the explicit recommendation to log sorting duration and units sorted per hour — so a manual-line equivalent of Availability × Performance × Quality can be computed from scheduled line hours, a target rate, a stoppage log and a rework count, with no machine signal at all ([iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/analytics-reporting/oee-loss-categorization-checklist-operators)). Second, **downtime logging and bonus fairness are the same feature**: separating non-attributable time is what makes a bonus defensible, and ReWear's raw-request and delivery timestamps already contain "sorter waiting for stock" as a derivable quantity. There is no published quality benchmark for a manual sorting line — no defect PPM, no rework rate comparable to the WERC accuracy figures — so targets must come from the site's own history.

The **shift leader (műszakvezető)** runs the shift, writes rota, allocates people and handles stoppages. The best-specified feature in the whole research set is theirs, and it is small. A published handover template specifies **six written fields completed in ten minutes or less before the outgoing supervisor leaves**: outgoing KPIs (output versus target, scrap, downtime minutes and primary cause); open issues each with an owner's name and a due date; equipment status; staffing notes; safety items; and a "must-know message" — one sentence on the single action the incoming supervisor must take before the line starts ([Sharpen Manufacturing](https://www.sharpenmfg.com/blog/shift-handoff-daily-management-template)). **Four of those six fields are not KPI data.** An application offering only a shift KPI summary covers one field of six; handover is a structured-notes-plus-owners feature, not a report. Two companion rules are equally cheap. The board must be current before the next shift's meeting, which means updating it belongs to shift **close**, not shift open — so the incoming supervisor's first screen should be *last shift*, not *today so far* ([TeamGuru](https://www.teamguru.com/guides/lean-daily-management)). And any issue open more than 48 hours without resolution should escalate automatically to the next tier "so the person who raised it doesn't have to push it" — which needs nothing more than a created timestamp, an owner and a state, all of which ReWear's to-do centre and notification badges already imply ([Sharpen Manufacturing](https://www.sharpenmfg.com/blog/shift-handoff-daily-management-template)). Timings across sources are conventions rather than standards (five to seven minutes in one, five to ten in another), and the tier cascade comes from practitioner blogs, not standards bodies.

The shift leader's other exposure is alerting. ReWear has automatic workflow notifications with live sidebar badges, which is exactly where alert fatigue accumulates. The warehouse literature has essentially no developed theory of operational alerting — the one warehouse-specific threshold found anywhere was a single vendor's "fill rate below 97% for a day", with no justification ([ASC Software](https://ascsoftware.com/blog/warehouse-kpis)) — so the transferable rules come from IT monitoring: every alert must be actionable ([OneUptime](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-01-24-fix-monitoring-alert-fatigue/view)); severity must determine the channel, with only critical items interrupting and everything else routed to dashboards or queued ([Icinga](https://icinga.com/blog/alert-fatigue-monitoring/)); and an always-ignored warning should be removed or converted into a dashboard metric rather than left firing. The strongest-provenance source is a peer-reviewed ACM survey of security operations centres finding that teams "struggle with fragmented and overloaded dashboards" ([ACM, 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3723158)). With one supervisor per shift and no on-call rota, the interrupt channel is extremely scarce: realistically only a stopped line, a missed dispatch cut-off, a stock-out on a committed order and a safety event earn it. It is worth noting that **no source names "dashboards nobody opens" as a warehouse anti-pattern** and no survey of warehouse dashboard usage was found; that concern is well attested in IT monitoring only.

The **warehouse manager (raktárvezető)** owns storage, locations, stocktake, inventory accuracy, capacity and dispatch. Their day is dominated by the question the WERC survey has moved towards: inbound control. In the 2026 top twelve, dock-to-stock cycle time ranks second, inventory count accuracy sixth, supplier-document accuracy eighth — having climbed from rank 30 — and on-time receipts tenth, up from eighteen, while capacity metrics dipped, which WERC reads as performance being "increasingly measured by how effectively inventory flows through the organization rather than how much the warehouse can store" ([Yale summary of WERC 2026](https://www.yale.com/4a4034/globalassets/coms/yale/north-america/documents/white-papers/yal-1558_werc2026updates_improvingdcmetricswhitepaper.pdf)). The benchmark values deserve real caution and should be treated as orientation, not targets: "best-in-class" means the **top 20% of survey respondents** and therefore moves annually, the WERC report itself is paywalled so every figure here arrives through Hyster-Yale's summaries, **no median values were obtainable at all**, and the respondent size distribution is unknown — so their applicability to a 10–50 person manual site is unestablished. A second vendor's conflicting figures (92.54% capacity, 99.9% count accuracy) disagree with the Yale/WERC 2025 numbers and should not be used ([RF-SMART](https://www.rfsmart.com/blog/warehouse-metrics-that-matter-for-netsuite-customers), contradicted by [Yale/WERC 2025](https://www.yale.com/globalassets/coms/yale/north-america/documents/white-papers/snack-drawer/YALE-1520_WERC-DC-Metrics-infographic.pdf)). The practical consequence is a design rule: KPI targets must be per-site editable fields with effective dates, never hard-coded thresholds.

One ordering point matters more than any individual metric. A WMS that directs product movement "requires extremely high inventory accuracy to be effective" ([Modern Materials Handling](https://www.mmh.com/article/rethink_cycle_counting_in_your_operations/features)). Counting is therefore a prerequisite for directed putaway and directed picking, not a successor to them — which means finishing the stocktake module (reopenable sessions, a printed count sheet, ABC stratification) comes *before* putaway suggestion rules and slotting, not after.

### Desk, compliance and administration: super admin, admin, HR/payroll

The **super admin** holds the permission matrix, the first-run setup and the backup and restore machinery. Their real job, read through the research, is to stop operational change from becoming a release. The recurring manager complaint about warehouse software is that it is "tough to implement changes", so routine changes — a new reason code, a changed target, a new report — become IT projects ([3PL Warehouse Manager reviews](https://www.softwareadvice.com/scm/3pl-warehouse-manager-profile/reviews/); [My Office Apps](https://www.myofficeapps.com/blog/best-warehouse-management-software/)). That collides directly with the daily-management requirement to review reason codes fortnightly and with EPR fee rates that change by decree. The resolution is a runtime reference-data layer with effective dates: EPR rates, tax types, KPI targets, grade definitions, downtime codes, bonus standards. A second super-admin responsibility is credential custody: the NAV machine interface needs a technical user with a signature key and an exchange key, and **only a primary user can create the technical user and generate those keys** on the web interface, so key rotation is an operational procedure the application must support rather than assume ([NAV EN v3.0 spec](https://onlineszamla.nav.gov.hu/api/files/container/download/Online%20Szamla_Interfesz%20specifikacio_EN_v3.0.pdf)).

The **admin** maintains customers, suppliers, locations, users and the category lists. Two of those become substantially heavier under the research. Customer records need a compliance dimension: a VIES-validated EU VAT number with the date and result of validation, because intra-EU exemption depends on the buyer holding and communicating a valid number, and the exemption **does not apply at all** where the supply was omitted or misreported in the A60 recapitulative statement unless the supplier can justify the error as made in good faith ([Adó Online](https://ado.hu/ado/valtoznak-az-eu-n-beluli-termekertekesites-adomentessegenek-feltetelei/)). From 21 May 2027 the same record needs a counterparty compliance register: permit or registration references, destination country, OECD status and a third-party environmentally-sound-management audit that must be **no more than two years old at the time of export** ([LG Fashion Labs](https://lgfashionlabs.com/eu-waste-shipment-regulation-textile-exports.html)). And the category list is not master data in the usual sense: with 90 to 500 categories negotiated per customer and per season, it is configuration that changes seasonally, which argues for versioned definitions with customer mappings rather than an enumeration.

**HR and payroll** write rota, maintain date-effective monthly and departmental daily rates, handle split shifts and approve bonuses. Their exposure is the most quantified in the entire compliance set. Hungarian labour inspection covers working-time records, and in the first half of 2026 **66% of 6,745 inspected employers had violations, with record-keeping errors affecting nearly 2,900 employees** ([BDO Hungary](https://www.bdo.hu/hu-hu/aktualitasok-blog/blog/kotetlen-munkaido-es-munkaido-nyilvantartas-mit-enged-meg-az-mt-,-es-mit-kell-mindenkepp-vezetni)). Mt. § 134 requires records of regular and extraordinary working time, standby and leave, and under § 134(2) the working time and the start and end of standby must be *naprakészen megállapíthatók* — determinable up to date — which means **recording daily hour totals is insufficient**. The simplified route under § 134(3) permits month-end written confirmation of the written schedule, but deviations must still be recorded currently and the simplification does not extend to standby. ReWear's rota is the *beosztás* (schedule) object that route depends on; what is missing is the actual record with start and end timestamps, the regular-versus-extraordinary split, standby with its own boundaries, and running overtime counters against the 250-hour annual cap plus up to 150 voluntary hours, or 300 plus 100 under a collective agreement.

The GDPR consequence sits in the same place and pulls the opposite way. Performance monitoring in Hungary rests on legitimate interest, which requires a documented *érdekmérlegelési teszt* (balancing test) that must itself form part of the privacy notice, plus prior written information under Mt. § 11/A(1) and GDPR Article 13 ([Adózóna](https://adozona.hu/gdpr_adatvedelem/GPS_nyomkovetes__mit_rogzithet_es_mit_nem_j_WIMIWT); [NAIH](https://naih.hu/files/2016_11_15_Tajekoztato_munkahelyi_adatkezelesek.pdf)). A NAIH decision found that giving only general information before the **trial operation** of a monitoring system was a violation, and publishing the detailed policy afterwards did not cure it — so a pilot is itself a risk point ([NAIH/2018/412/H](https://www.naih.hu/files/NAIH-2018-412-H_hatarozat.pdf)). Because granular monitoring data should have short enforced retention while payroll-linked accounting documents must be kept at least **eight years** ([Netlock](https://netlock.com/hu/tudastar/elektronikus-archivalas)), the two data sets have to be architecturally separate: a statutory working-time record that is append-only and long-lived, and a productivity-analytics store that is deletable. Several questions here are genuinely unresolved and need a lawyer rather than more searching: the statutory retention period for working-time records is contested between three and five years with the Act itself apparently silent; works council (*üzemi tanács*) information and consultation duties over introducing monitoring **could not be sourced at all**; no NAIH decision or fine specifically about productivity tracking was found, so the camera and GPS reasoning is applied by analogy; and whether a DPIA is mandatory was not checked, though it is highly likely to be relevant.

## If only five things get built next, build these in this order

**1. Exact-decimal weights (small to medium).** Smallest of the five and a precondition for three of the others. The developer has already made this exact argument once, for money. Weight carries an EPR penalty of half the fee deficiency, a 100 g stocktake tolerance and contractual bale tolerances of a few percent; floating point in a kilogram-priced business is a control failure, not an aesthetic one.

**2. A real NAV Online Számla integration, replacing the demonstration (medium to large).** This has the largest downside of anything on the list and no discretionary component. Invoicing software must transmit **immediately on issue, in XML, without manual intervention**, and the obligation is met only once NAV confirms successful processing — an ERROR response means the invoice is simply not reported. The penalty is **up to HUF 1,000,000 per invoice** since 1 August 2024, and the validation regime tightened on 15 September 2025 when **15 former warnings became blocking errors** ([BDO Hungary](https://www.bdo.hu/hu-hu/aktualitasok-blog/blog/nav-online-szamla-adatszolgaltatas-kotelezettsegek,-hataridok,-bejovo-szamlak)). Concretely: a per-invoice reporting state machine (pending → submitted with `transactionId` → DONE / WARN / ERROR / ABORTED), the `transactionId` and response code stored against the invoice as audit evidence, automatic retry on outage, an operator queue and an alert path for errors, and `ANNUL` modelled separately from commercial cancellation because technical annulment requires human approval in NAV's own web interface. Build the invoice data model separably from the transport format now, so the same invoice can be emitted as NAV XML today and as an EN 16931 structured e-invoice when ViDA's cross-border reporting starts on 1 July 2030.

**3. The purchase lot and the intake assessment record (large).** This is the schema keystone and it unlocks the next item, the export document pack, the Y46 classification chain and EPR attribution. One lot entity carrying supplier, country of origin, collection channel, purchase price per kilogram, gross and net weight, customs classification, and the named human assessment of whether the material is waste or fit for re-use at intake. It also retires the acknowledged weakness that collection is recorded by management with the area as free text.

**4. Grade-yield reporting, and a target that uses it (medium to large).** With cream at roughly a tenth of weight and over half of revenue, this is the measurement the business actually runs on. Report yield by grade per intake lot, per team, per shift and per sorter, with purchase and sale prices as editable inputs so the margin per lot is visible. Pair it with the downtime log, because the two together are what make a bonus figure defensible as well as what make a lot's profitability legible.

**5. The shift handover record with 48-hour escalation (small).** The cheapest high-value item in the research. Six structured fields at shift close, four of which are not KPI data, plus automatic escalation of anything open past 48 hours using the notification infrastructure that already exists. This is also the most reliable way to displace a spreadsheet, since spreadsheets survive wherever the system holds the data but not the working artefact the manager needs ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/scm/warehouse-management-system-comparison/)).

**One conditional swap, stated explicitly.** If the operation ships outside the EU — and a USD ledger suggests it does — then the Article 22d(9) bale record and the export document pack displace item five, because **21 May 2027** is a hard date for the export rules and a handover sheet is not. The mobile client deliberately does not appear in the five: it is the largest item on the roadmap, it brings the whole offline-synchronisation problem with it, and its evidence base is a mechanism and a preference survey rather than a measured cost. It is the right sixth item, not the right first.

## Conclusion

The instinct when auditing a custom warehouse application against commercial products is to count missing modules, and that instinct would mislead here. ReWear is not behind because it lacks wave picking, cartonization, RFID or demand forecasting; at this scale and in this trade, each of those is a correct omission, and the published evidence for several of them at 10–50 staff is either absent or openly contradictory. It is behind in two narrow places with wide consequences. The first is that its data model treats intake as internally generated collection with a free-text area, when the published picture of a Central European sorter is a buyer of heterogeneous imported feedstock whose provenance now carries legal weight — and a legal status set by a human judgement at the moment of collection that the application cannot currently record. The second is that it measures the wrong thing: kilograms processed, in a business where a tenth of the weight carries half the revenue.

The deeper pattern worth carrying forward is that in this operation the compliance artefacts and the management artefacts are the same artefacts. A bale record with an identified weight, a declared grade, a composition and a traceable link to the shift that made it is simultaneously the Article 22d(9) document, the evidence that defends a grading dispute, the input to the EPR declaration and the row in a grade-yield report. A downtime log with short supervisor-maintained reason codes is simultaneously the manual-line OEE denominator and the thing that makes a bonus figure survive a challenge. That convergence is unusual and it is good news for a small development team, because it means the highest-value work is not a long list of separate features — it is a small number of records made properly. The honest counterweight is that much of the evidence supporting the detail of these recommendations is thin: no measured cost for desktop-only systems, no independent throughput figure for a European manual sorting line, no benchmark for quality on a sorting line, no Hungarian yield data, no current grade prices, no published grading standard, no sourced pay or turnover data, and a textile EPR deductibility question that no source answered. Build the records so that the site can measure itself, and let the operation supply the numbers the literature cannot.
